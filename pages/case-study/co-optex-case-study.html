Co-optex — Digital Commerce & Retail POS

Designing connected experiences across digital commerce and retail operations.

A UI/UX design project spanning the Co-optex e-commerce experience and Infotex POS interface, covering customer-facing shopping journeys, retail operations, inventory, sales, customer workflows and reporting.

Complex workflows, data and operational interfaces

The project

The Co-optex work covered two connected digital experiences: the customer-facing e-commerce platform and the Infotex POS interface supporting retail operations.

My UI/UX contribution spanned product-focused web experiences as well as operational screens for sales, inventory, customer workflows, collections and reporting.

The two environments required different approaches to information hierarchy, interaction patterns and visual density while keeping each experience clear and consistent for its intended users.

Customer-facing experience

  • Product discovery and browsing
  • Catalogue and product presentation
  • Product information and shopping actions
  • Responsive digital commerce interfaces

Retail operations

  • Sales and transaction workflows
  • Customer-related operations
  • Inventory and stock workflows
  • Collections and reporting interfaces

The challenge

Co-optex involved two very different interaction environments. The e-commerce experience needed to support product discovery and purchase decisions, while Infotex POS needed to support detailed retail operations and transaction-heavy workflows.

The design challenge was to provide the right level of information and interaction for each context without making either experience harder to understand.

Different users. Different priorities.

  • Help shoppers discover and understand textile products.
  • Organise product categories and information clearly.
  • Support transaction-oriented retail workflows.
  • Structure inventory and customer operations.
  • Present operational records and reports clearly.

Design strategy

Rather than applying the same interface approach to every screen, the work was shaped around the context of each experience.

The e-commerce side prioritised product visibility, discovery and shopping actions. The POS side required stronger information hierarchy, operational clarity and support for complex workflows.

01

Clarity

Make important information and actions easy to identify within each workflow.

02

Context

Design differently for customer-facing discovery and operational retail tasks.

03

Consistency

Use familiar patterns across related screens and workflows to reduce unnecessary complexity.

Co-optex e-commerce experience

The customer-facing experience focused on helping users discover textile products, browse the catalogue and evaluate products before moving toward a purchase.

The interface places the product experience at the centre while keeping navigation, supporting information and shopping actions clear throughout the journey.

Co-optex — customer-facing digital commerce interface.

E-commerce journey

Discover → Browse → Evaluate → Purchase

The shopping experience can be viewed as a connected journey, with each stage helping users understand the available products and decide what to do next.

1

Discover

Reach products through navigation, categories and visual presentation.

2

Browse

Explore available products through structured catalogue content.

3

Evaluate

Review product information before choosing the next action.

4

Purchase

Keep relevant shopping actions visible as the user progresses.

Product-first hierarchy

Product imagery and information receive visual priority so users can understand what is available before deciding how to proceed.

Clear discovery

Catalogue structure and navigation help users move from broad product discovery toward more focused product exploration.

Visible shopping actions

Important actions remain understandable at the points where users need to make a shopping decision.

Infotex POS

The project also covered the broader Infotex POS interface used for retail operations.

Unlike the customer-facing commerce experience, POS users work with transactions, customer records, inventory, collections and reports. The interface therefore needed to support information-dense workflows and frequent operational actions.

Infotex POS — dashboard providing a structured entry point to retail operations.

POS workflow

Access → Operate → Review → Report

The POS environment brings together multiple operational areas. The interface needs to let users reach the relevant function, complete a task, review information and work with operational reports.

1

Access

Reach the required operational area through the system structure.

2

Operate

Complete sales, customer, inventory and related retail tasks.

3

Review

Work with transaction records, forms, lists and operational details.

4

Report

Review operational information through reporting interfaces.

Navigation and operational structure

The POS system contains multiple functional areas and workflows. A structured menu provides the starting point for moving through these operational functions.

Infotex POS — navigation structure for the wider retail operations system.

Sales workflow

Sales represents one of the primary operational workflows within the POS interface. The screen needs to bring transaction information and available actions together in a usable working area.

New Sales — transaction-focused retail workflow.

Transaction management

Retail operations extend beyond creating a sale. Return processing and bill search introduce additional workflows where users need to work with existing transaction information.

Sales Return — transaction workflow.

Bill Search — operational record lookup.

Customer and inventory operations

The POS experience extends into customer and inventory workflows, adding another layer of operational information beyond the immediate transaction.

Customer workflow — structured customer information.

Current Stock Status — inventory-related operational workflow.

Beyond the core transaction

The wider POS interface also includes supporting operational workflows such as stock inward, inventory closing, gift coupon entry and customer-related activities.

Stock Inward — inventory workflow.

Inventory Closing — operational stock workflow.

Gift Coupon Entry — supporting retail workflow.

Customer Details — structured customer information.

Operational reporting

Reporting screens introduce a higher level of information density. The interface needs to organise records and business information in a way that supports review and interpretation.

Reporting interface — structured operational information.

Sales reporting — transaction-oriented information.

Sales report — operational review interface.

Design process

Across the e-commerce and POS experiences, the interface work followed a workflow-first approach: understand the task, structure the information, establish the interaction pattern and then refine the visual interface.

01

Understand

Understand the business workflow, information and task requirements.

02

Structure

Organise navigation, information, fields and actions around the workflow.

03

Design & refine

Translate the structure into consistent, responsive and implementation-ready UI.

Key design decisions

Information hierarchy

Customer-facing screens prioritise products and shopping actions, while POS screens place stronger emphasis on transaction information, forms, records and operational actions.

Data-dense interfaces

Operational screens contain more information than a typical consumer shopping interface. Grouping related information and maintaining familiar structures helps users work through these screens.

Task-oriented interaction

Each operational screen is organised around the task being completed, keeping relevant information and actions together.

Consistent patterns

Familiar navigation, forms, lists, tables, filters and action areas provide a consistent interaction language across related workflows.

Inside the Infotex POS interface

The POS work extended across a broad collection of operational screens. The following selection represents different areas of the interface and demonstrates the range of workflows covered.

Stock Inward List

RO Contract Collection

Discount Certification

Credit Customer Registration

Credit Customer Registration Form

Daily Transaction Statement

Interface language

Across the operational interface, repeated patterns help establish familiarity between related workflows. Navigation, forms, tables, status information and actions follow a structured visual language so users can focus on the task rather than relearning the interface.

Outcome

The Co-optex work brought together two complementary retail experiences: a customer-facing digital commerce platform and a broad operational POS interface.

The work demonstrates my ability to design across different users, workflows and levels of information density — from product discovery and shopping interfaces to transaction, inventory and reporting workflows.

The focus throughout was on making information easier to understand, actions easier to identify and workflows easier to navigate.

Reflection

Working across e-commerce and POS strengthened my understanding of how strongly UX depends on user context.

A shopper needs visual clarity, product information and straightforward decisions, while a retail operator needs fast access to detailed information and frequent operational actions.

This project reinforced my approach to information hierarchy, transactional workflows, data-dense interfaces and reusable UI patterns.

Good enterprise UX is about creating clear structure around the user's task while respecting the complexity of the workflow.
